## Access / infra gotchas
- Sandbox egress: the proxy injects the APIYI key; a wrong secret shows as `X-Proxy-Error: upstream auth failed`
  + `401 Invalid token`. `docs.apiyi.com` and H3's result host `*.myqcloud.com` (Tencent COS) had to be allowed.
- H3 result URLs are public, unsigned COS links; they can be fed straight back into H3 as `reference_video`
  without downloading (that is how E2b used the mannequin clip).
- jsDelivr pinned links `.../gh/user/repo@<sha>/path` are REJECTED by H3 at submission (400 "must be a public
  HTTPS URL") — the `@` trips its URL validator; `%40` is not served by jsDelivr. raw.githubusercontent.com was
  accepted once and then failed three tasks with `input_download_failed ... network is unreachable` (H3 reaches it
  over IPv6). What works: jsDelivr WITHOUT a version (`cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/user/repo/path`, default branch).
  Not pinned, so the rule becomes: never overwrite a reference file; a new version gets a new filename.
- Failed H3 tasks (e.g. media download) are refunded; 400 at submission is not billed.

## Image models (APIYI, live prices 2026-10-12)

## Character design (E1, E1b)
- Single image: flash ≈ pro. Sheet of 8 poses in one call: pro is consistent, flash drifted the fox onto four legs
  on 2/8 figures. Mood board + sheet in one image: worse on both (drew ground lines, quadrupeds) — dropped.
- FIX for body-plan drift: say the body plan in capitals and forbid the alternative, then repeat it per pose
  ("ANTHROPOMORPHIC, BIPEDAL … front legs are ARMS … NEVER on all fours, in every single figure"). With that,
  24/24 figures upright, even on flash. Passing the approved design as an identity reference then locks the look,
  and flash+identity-ref ≈ pro+identity-ref → pay for pro once (the design), use flash for everything after.
- The model ignores "fill 65 %, generous margin" (owl filled the frame) and "strict side view" for a round owl.
  Pad the reference yourself before handing it to video.
- Key colour: magenta for the orange fox (pale-pink inner ears survive: key excess min(R,B)−G ≈ 0 on pink);
  green for the lavender owl. Both keyed clean first time with lo=40, hi=120.

## Post-processing settings that worked
- key.py: magenta/green excess key, lo 40 hi 120, despill = subtract the excess. `--key border` for odd backgrounds.
- cut_sheet.py: dilate 4 px before labelling, min area 4000 px (drops motion marks, specks).
- pack.py: baseline = lowest opaque row; x pivot = head centroid (turnarounds) or feet (walks/idles); ONE scale for
  the whole set (a crouch must not grow back); height 160 px for this game.
- video_frames.py: 24 fps extraction, seam search on premultiplied 96 px thumbnails normalised by median
  frame-to-frame motion (score < 1 ≈ seamless), 24 frames per loop for a 4 s idle.
- onion.py numbers: idle IoU > 0.9, walk 0.75–0.9; area within a few %; flag shifts > 3 px.

## Round 3 (E7 standard set, 16-frame drawn sheets, size fix, mannequin library)
- Mannequins (text-to-video, $0.12 each) performed 4, 6 AND 8 actions in 4 s, all recognisable; at 8 each is
  sketchier (jump = bounce, stand-up = crouch). The mannequin is not the limit at 8.
- Fox driven by them: 8/4 s was the cleanest (background held, sit/stand/turn good, run reads as a walk, first
  0.5 s zooms in from the reference framing); 4/4 s got "speed rings" in the background; 6/4 s went pastel with
  rings and zoomed. Background stability is luck (1 of 3), so budget a retry per clip.
- One character set needs ONE scale across strips: `pack.py --src-scale 0.25` (output px per source px), never
  fit-to-height per strip (a sitting strip would be blown up to standing size).
- Despill must touch only edge pixels (alpha < 0.98): pale opaque fur has a small positive key excess and turned
  green-tinted.
- Mannequin library: `refs/mannequins/*.mp4` (audio stripped, 230–530 KB), referenced through jsDelivr — H3
  accepts video from jsDelivr (unversioned) too.
- Drawn 16-frame sheets (4×4, 1:1, $0.0245): smooth subtle idle for the fox, excellent 16-beat doze for the owl.
- Drawn standard set (4×4, one action per row): walk and sit/stand read well, run is a brisk walk on both models,
  jumps small. Cut action rows with `cut_sheet.py --grid 4x4` (keeps height in the cell) + `--anchor fixed`.
- `--fix-scale` (best-fit overlay on frame 1): idles only. On pose actions it "corrects" real change (crouch +15 %,
  sit −11 %).
- Loops cut on the clock snap at the seam (owner: "end state jumps to start" on the run). `segments.py
  NAME=t0-t1::loop` searches the window for the best-matching pair of frames ≥ --min-cycle apart and keeps one
  whole stride: run 1.0–1.75 s seam 3.03 → 0.958–1.667 s seam 0.65 (seam IoU 0.82 vs median step 0.86).
  A 0.5 s walk slot holds one step only (best seam 2.31): give locomotion ≥ 1 s per action in the mannequin
  (two strides), or a dedicated walk/run mannequin.
- Owner's rule after E7: one second per pose. Price check for 8 poses: one 8 s clip = two 4 s clips = $0.24
  (H3 bills requested seconds only; reference media free). Two 4 s clips win on expected cost (a failed clip is
  re-run whole: $0.12 vs $0.24, and 1 of 3 clips had a broken background), on evidence (the only 8 s clip barely
  moved), and on library reuse. Their one cost is a second ~0.5 s start-up settle — lead each clip with idle.

## Round 5 — cutout puppets (C1, 2026-10-13, $0.23)

Nano Banana draws a character taken apart into puppet pieces on the first try (exploded view or grid), in its
own style, and draws the hidden overlap at joints when asked ("extend each piece with a plain rounded tab past
the joint"). Pro drew joint tabs by itself on the owl (usable as pivots; erase them where they would show).
`skill/cutout/scripts/pieces.py` keys and splits a sheet by connected component; rigs in `rigs/*.json` (pivot +
attach point per piece, sheet pixels); `skill/cutout/player/cutout.js` plays procedural actions on a canvas.
Owl (round body): excellent. Fox (biped, 3/4): walk reads at game size, rigid up close; draw order is fixed per
piece, so the near arm cannot wave without covering the face. All actions for one character: 43–48 KB of art,
against 117 KB for one 24-frame video idle. Review page: `review/cutout/index.html`
(https://claude.ai/artifact/GtS15Z5WAL6E1eJmsCyuni). Not yet a skill: rigging was done by eye.

## Round 6 — cutout from the reference picture itself (C2, $0.147)

C1's quality gap came from drawing pieces separately (each piece outlined, hat floating, mismatched edges).
C2 takes the finished reference apart instead, all with Nano Banana edits of that same picture:
a flat-colour parts map (lines up to the original: silhouette overlap 0.992), "remove X and paint what was under
it" fills, and face edits (blink, sing: overlap 0.998, so they are cut where they differ and laid on top).
`skill/cutout/scripts/decompose.py SPEC OUT` builds the layers, registers fills that came back scaled
(body edit: scale 0.78, overlap 0.95), feathers inner seams (head into body), chokes the outer key edge
(no fringe on dark grounds), and puts each pivot at the centre of the contact edge with the parent.
Misses: "remove the wing" was ignored in 2 of 3 edits that asked for it together with other changes —
ask for one removal per edit. A rigid wing keeps its root on the body when it flaps.
Layer order and hidden areas (user review of C2): (1) a fill that removes a part paints whatever it likes under it
(the "no hat" edit drew a second ear tuft) — keep only a band of the hidden area next to the visible part
(`covered_band`), or the part above sits "on the ear". (2) Draw order is not seg order: parts are disjoint at
rest, but a moving wing must draw above the head's side fur, or it slides under it and reads as "behind the back".
(3) Limbs and wings pivot at the upper INNER corner (`shoulder_right/left`), not the middle of the top edge.
(4) A mirrored far wing must sit fully behind the body at rest.
