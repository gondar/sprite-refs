A tall, elegant grey heron musician, full body from the top of his hat to his feet, three-quarter view facing to the RIGHT. Calm, dignified, a little melancholic, artistically refined — not cute, not cartoonish, no big eyes. Long slender S-curved neck, long sharp dark-grey beak pointing right, a black crest plume from the eye backwards, a small golden eye with a black pupil, closed beak.
He wears a wide shallow conical hat of woven straw (Japanese kasa) and a shaggy straw rain cape (mino) made of overlapping layers of cut straw-coloured paper, tied at the throat with a black cord bow. Grey-blue wing feathers come out from under the cape as arms; the wing-tip feathers work like fingers.
He holds a small light-brown wooden ukulele across his chest in playing position: its neck points up and to the right, the right-hand wing-tip holds the neck, the other wing-tip rests just above the sound hole, ready to strum.
He stands on ONE long thin dark-grey leg with a clawed foot flat on the ground; the other leg is drawn up and tucked under the cape. Feet at the bottom of the picture, nothing under them.
Style: layered hand-cut paper art — Japanese washi and chiyogami paper with visible fibres and torn edges, Polish Kurpie paper cut-out details, shin-hanga woodblock print feel. Palette: ink black, warm grey, washi cream, straw ochre, small touches of aizome indigo and vermilion (a vermilion cord knot, an indigo band on the hat). No green anywhere on the character.
The whole character sits centred on a completely FLAT, uniform, plain background of solid colour #22B24C, with clear space around him on every side (the hat and the feet must not touch the edges). No ground, no rock, no shadow, no scenery, no text, no frame, no border.
