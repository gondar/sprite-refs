SCENE: The Scourging at the Pillar, told symbolically and gently: a quiet stone courtyard in Jerusalem at night, the moment of waiting.

Jesus, a thin man of about thirty with long dark hair and a short beard, in a plain undyed homespun tunic, stands beside a single rough grey stone column, a little left of centre. His wrists are tied together in front of him with a coarse rope whose end is looped loosely around the column. His head is bowed, eyes lowered, his face calm and patient. His dark crimson-red cloak lies dropped in a heap on the flagstones at the foot of the column. He is unhurt and fully clothed.

The soldiers are NOT in the picture: only their two long blue-black SHADOWS fall across the cracked plaster wall on the right, tall stretched shadows of two men in helmets standing at a distance, cast by the torch; their owners are outside the frame.

The place: a poor walled courtyard of cracked mud plaster and rough stone, worn uneven flagstones, a low stone step along the right wall, a wooden bucket, a coil of frayed rope on the ground. One torch burns in an iron ring on the left wall; it makes a single warm pool of light on Jesus and the column, while the rest of the courtyard is deep indigo. Above the wall a deep indigo night sky with a pale moon partly behind spiral clouds.

Composition: the column and Jesus are left of centre; the right third is the plain wall with the long shadows; the lower part of the frame is open empty flagstones, with the low stone step on the lower right.
