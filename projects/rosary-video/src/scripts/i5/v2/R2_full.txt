SCENE: The Visitation, in the hill village of Ain Karem in the morning, just after sunrise.

Mary has walked for many days through the hills. She is a thin village girl of about fourteen, barefoot with dusty feet, in a plain faded blue-grey homespun dress with a sewn patch at the hem, its hem dirty brown with road dust, a rough undyed head cloth slipping back from her hair; she is tired, a walking stick has fallen from her hand. Her small travel bundle, a knotted coarse cloth, has been DROPPED on the ground behind her and lies on its side by the path, a little loaf spilling out.

Elizabeth, her old cousin, a very old, wrinkled, bent woman, HEAVILY PREGNANT with a big round belly, in a worn dark brown wool dress and a grey wool head cloth, has just come out of the LOW dark doorway of her house. She has grabbed Mary with both arms and holds her, laughing with tears running down her wrinkled cheeks, her eyes closed with joy. Mary is leaning into her embrace, her head on Elizabeth's shoulder, her eyes closed, exhausted and happy. Their arms are around each other, but their heads and their bodies above the waist keep a little dark space between them where the doorway shows.

The place: an old poor house of rough grey and ochre fieldstone, with a crooked flat roof of beams and packed earth, a low small dark doorway with a worn wooden door on the right of centre, a clay jar by the wall, a frayed rope. Behind, dry terraced hills with stony fields, a few gnarled olive trees with twisting spiral trunks and spiral-shaped silvery-dull leaves, a thin wind line curling as a spiral. The sun has just risen behind the hill on the left: low, pale amber, partly hidden, long cool shadows across everything; the sky is a dull hazy dawn grey-ochre. Dusty dry grass, thistles, stones.

Composition: the two women stand off-centre, a little right of the middle, at the house corner, caught mid-embrace; the dusty path winds in from the left across the lower third, open and empty bare earth with Mary's footprints, a few stones only at its edges.

Do not copy their content. Take the low, quiet key of Image 1 (deep shadow, one glowing light source), not the brightness of Image 2.

STYLE: a flat 2D hand-drawn animation background in the visual language of Cartoon Saloon's The Secret of Kells and Wolfwalkers: large flat areas of colour, strong readable silhouettes, textured gouache fills with paper grain, slightly flattened perspective. Spirals live only in nature and light (tree trunks, olive leaves, wind, the rays of light), never as ornament on clothes or buildings. Figures are angular and stylised in the Kells manner: small heads on long necks, almond eyes with dot pupils, tiny mouths, long slender hands, simple clothes drawn as plain flat shapes.

LIGHT AND PALETTE: LOW KEY, dim and earthy. Early dawn half-light: most of the picture sits in deep cool shadow (umber, dusty brown, slate blue, dark ochre, olive), with ONE warm light source making a single pool of soft amber light. Muted, desaturated, quiet colours like old clay and wool. Few flowers or none. No pastel, no mint green, no pink, no bright sky.

POVERTY AND SIMPLICITY: everything is poor, worn and handmade: rough uneven stone, cracked mud plaster, bare packed earth, coarse undyed wool, patched cloth, chipped clay pots. Nothing ornamental, nothing gold, no jewellery, no patterned borders on clothing.

COMPOSITION: candid and asymmetric, slightly off-centre, as if the camera caught a passing moment; the figures are not posed for the viewer and do not look at the viewer; objects lie where they were dropped or used. Wide 16:9, the painted world fills the canvas edge to edge. Each figure is a readable silhouette against the background. Only the people described appear; no animals, no birds.

Hard exclusions: no text, no letters, no signature, no watermark, no frame, no border, full-bleed image.
