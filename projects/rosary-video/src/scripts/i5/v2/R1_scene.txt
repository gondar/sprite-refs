SCENE: The Annunciation, in a poor one-room house in the village of Nazareth at early dawn, before sunrise.

The room: rough walls of uneven fieldstone and cracked mud plaster, a low ceiling of dark wooden beams and reeds, a packed dirt floor. On the left, a worn wooden door stands half open, and the cold blue-grey dawn comes in through it. A small clay oil lamp burns on a stone ledge, the only warm light. A coarse dark goat-hair curtain hangs crooked across a corner on the right. A tall clay water jar with a chipped lip leans by the wall, a frayed woven reed mat lies crumpled on the floor, a broom of twigs rests against the wall.

Mary was KNEADING DOUGH: she kneels on the dirt floor, slightly right of centre, over a low shallow wooden kneading trough with a lump of dough in it and a scatter of flour on the floor; a small sack of flour sags open beside it. She is a simple village girl of about fourteen, thin, barefoot, in a plain faded blue-grey homespun dress with a darker sewn patch on the sleeve, a rough undyed head cloth tied back loosely, a few strands of dark hair escaping. Her sleeves are pushed up; her hands and forearms are white with flour, one hand still pressed into the dough. She has just turned her head sharply over her shoulder toward the angel, eyes wide, startled but listening, mouth slightly open.

The angel: left of centre, coming in from the open doorway, a tall soft figure made of the pale dawn light itself, almost translucent, a simple long shape of faint gold-white light without any costume details; his wings are long sweeping spirals of light rays that blend into the morning light streaming through the door. He bends low, almost folded over, his head down beside Mary's ear, whispering, one long hand raised near his mouth. A narrow gap of dark room shows between his face and Mary's head.

Composition: the two figures sit in the middle band, a little off-centre to the right, cropped casually; the lower third in front of them is open bare dirt floor in soft shadow with only a few crumbs of flour and a dropped wooden spoon near the edge, leaving a clear empty floor strip.
