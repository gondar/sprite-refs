SCENE: Pentecost, the coming of the Holy Spirit, in a simple upper room at dawn.

A poor, plain upper room with rough mud-plaster walls, a low beamed ceiling, a bare wooden floor, a worn mat, a clay water jar, a low rough bench. On the right an open window opening with a plain undyed linen curtain blowing hard into the room; through the window the peach-apricot glow of dawn and a cool blue-grey sky. A rushing wind drawn as soft white spiral lines sweeps through the room, lifting a few loose sheets of plain parchment off the floor into the air.

Mary, a simple young woman in a faded blue-grey homespun dress and a rough undyed head cloth, sits on the low bench a little left of centre, her hands open on her lap, looking up with a quiet, joyful smile. Around her, with a little space between each, three apostles in rough brown and grey patched homespun: one older bearded man stands with his arms half opened in astonishment; one young man laughs with joy, holding on to his head cloth in the wind; one kneels on the mat, looking up, eyes wide. Above EACH head floats one small, soft tongue of flame made of warm light, gently glowing, lighting each face from above.

Light: the single main light is the soft white-gold glow of the small flames and the wind light; the room's corners stay in cool blue-grey shadow.

Composition: candid and asymmetric; the figures in the middle band, slightly off-centre to the left. The lower-right foreground is an open stretch of bare wooden floor with a dropped sheet of parchment and the clay jar near the wall, calm and free of figures.
