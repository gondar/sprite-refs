A storybook illustration in the style of Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac: pen-and-ink line work with muted, translucent watercolor washes, of a European hazel dormouse as a character design. The dormouse sits upright on a twisting hazel branch holding a hazelnut in both tiny front paws. It is drawn accurately: a small round body with golden-ginger fur, a pale cream throat, very large dark glossy eyes, small rounded ears, fine long whiskers, and a long, evenly furred, softly bushy tail that curls behind it.
Behind it lies an overgrown walled rose garden: crumbling old brick wall, gnarled thorny rose vines with pale dusty-rose blooms, knotted roots and tree trunks with faces hidden in the bark the way Rackham drew them, mist in the distance.
Palette of sepia, olive green, faded ochre, dusty rose and parchment, with a warm aged-paper texture. Wiry, nervous, expressive ink line; mature, mysterious, slightly eerie fairy-tale atmosphere of an Edwardian illustrated book, delicate and naturalistic rather than cute cartoon. Medium shot, dormouse in the left third of the frame. No text, no signature, no watermark. 16:9 landscape.
