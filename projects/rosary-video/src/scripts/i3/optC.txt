Immersive view from inside a giant hazelnut half-shell; the broken shell rim curves past close to the camera around all edges, dark and softly out of focus, the shell filling the entire frame with no paper margin. Set into the hollow of the shell, like a Gothic boxwood prayer nut (a sixteenth-century Flemish micro-carving), is the Nativity carved as a tiny, intricate relief in warm honey-coloured boxwood: a pointed Gothic arch with delicate carved tracery and tiny pinnacles frames the scene; beneath it a small stable with a carved thatched roof; Mary kneeling on the left with folded hands, the Infant Jesus swaddled in a manger in the centre, Joseph standing on the right with a staff, an ox and a donkey behind the manger, a carved star above. Everything — figures, arch, roof, straw — is carved from the same single piece of pale golden wood, with fine chisel marks, deep tiny shadows in the recesses and soft polished highlights on the rounded forms; the figures' faces are small, smooth and peaceful.

Lighting: soft warm golden light falling from above onto the carving, the shell rim in shadow. Still, reverent, precious, like looking at a tiny treasure.

The holy figures are simple, gentle and quiet, slightly flat like figures in an old storybook or a small icon: calm oval faces with closed or lowered eyes, soft simple robes, no exaggerated or grotesque features.
Style: an Arthur Rackham-style illustration, fine sepia pen-and-ink linework with delicate cross-hatching and muted transparent watercolor washes on warm aged, slightly foxed paper; palette of sepia, olive green, ochre, dusty rose and soft grey-blue. Mature, mysterious, poetic storybook mood, not cute and not cartoon.
