Immersive view from INSIDE a giant hazelnut half-shell, as if the camera has flown into it: the concave inner wall of the shell fills the ENTIRE frame from edge to edge, curving around the viewer like the inside of a small dome or apse, with fine wood-grain fibres of pale honey and brown shell texture. All around the outer edges of the picture (top, bottom, left and right corners) the thick, jagged broken rim of the brown shell curves past close to the camera, dark and softly out of focus, forming an irregular oval vignette. No paper margin, no outside background: everything in the frame is shell.

On the concave inner wall, painted directly onto the shell like a small old fresco or a Byzantine-inspired icon on a dome, is the Nativity: in the centre the Infant Jesus swaddled in white lying in a simple manger of straw, with a soft golden halo; Mary kneeling on the left in a dusty blue-grey mantle, hands folded in prayer; Joseph standing on the right in an ochre-brown cloak, leaning on a staff; behind them an ox and a donkey peeking gently over the manger, and a single eight-pointed star above shining a thin ray down. The fresco follows the curvature of the wall, with slightly faded, cracked plaster-like pigment, gold-leaf halos softly worn.

Lighting: warm candle-like golden glow coming from the centre of the fresco, deep soft brown shadow toward the rim. Quiet, reverent, intimate.

The holy figures are simple, gentle and quiet, slightly flat like figures in an old storybook or a small icon: calm oval faces with closed or lowered eyes, soft simple robes, no exaggerated or grotesque features.
Style: an Arthur Rackham-style illustration, fine sepia pen-and-ink linework with delicate cross-hatching and muted transparent watercolor washes on warm aged, slightly foxed paper; palette of sepia, olive green, ochre, dusty rose and soft grey-blue. Mature, mysterious, poetic storybook mood, not cute and not cartoon.
The painting has no signature, no monogram, no lettering and no text anywhere. No border frame.
